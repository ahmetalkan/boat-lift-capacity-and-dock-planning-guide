Water Depth, Tidal Range and Shoreline Permit Checks

A lift cradle sits beneath the boat's deepest keel or drive skeg. If water depth drops during winter reservoir drawdowns or coastal spring low tides, an under-checked site leaves the boat stranded on dry bunks or stuck above shallow mud.

Site checks before selecting lift architecture

  • Minimum usable depth formula: Boat draft (with drive trimmed up) + height of lift cradle beam and bunks + comfortable mud/sand clearance at the lowest water level you plan to launch in. Use NOAA Tides and Currents stations for tidal shorelines.
  • Bottom substrate check: Soft muck, sand, or rocky ledge governs whether a freestanding lake lift, four-post piling lift, or seawall-mounted elevator lift fits the slip.
  • Permitting sequence: Coastal zones, wetlands, and navigable waterways typically require local marine-contractor permitting and HOA/harbor approval before ordering custom pilings.
