Boat Lift Cable Inspection and Seasonal Upkeep

Saltwater corrosion and uneven cable winding shorten lift hardware life quickly if ignored. A five-minute inspection each month catches minor wear before a cradle drops unevenly.

Routine inspection points

  • Watch cable winding every time: Never lower the cradle onto the bottom so cables go slack—slack wire overlaps on the winder pipe, kinks ("birdcages"), and weakens rapidly.
  • Check stainless cable strands visually: Broken wires, rust staining, or flattened kinks mean immediate cable replacement before hoisting the boat again.
  • Grease pulley sheaves and check zinc anodes: Lubricate grease fittings on schedule, rinse saltwater off beams and cradles with fresh water, and replace sacrificial zinc anodes before they dissolve away.
  • Pull the boat's bilge drain plug on the lift: Rainwater filling an uncovered hull on a lift adds thousands of pounds rapidly—pull the garboard plug when stored dry, and always double-check it is back in before launching.
